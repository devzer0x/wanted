## GTA V Story Mode — HUD & Radar Legend

**Radar / minimap basics**
- The minimap ("radar") sits in the bottom-left of the screen and rotates/pans as you move; it shows your immediate surroundings, streets, and nearby blips.
- The player is shown as an arrow/marker at the center of the radar, pointing in your facing direction.
- Blips render as colored circles; when a blip is above or below your current elevation, a small arrow appears inside the circle pointing up or down.
- In a vehicle the radar switches to a slanted/perspective view; on foot it's flatter. Entering a building swaps the radar to a floor-plan view of that interior.
- Health, armor, and (in single-player) the Special Ability meter are drawn as bars wrapped along the bottom edge of the radar, not as a separate HUD block.

**Blip colors — what they mean**
- **Yellow** = a fixed mission/location point — this matches the game's own on-screen hint that "locations are marked with a yellow dot on the Radar."
- **Red** = an enemy or hostile threat.
- **Blue / green** = a friendly object or character relevant to the current objective (exact hue varies by context, not a strict "all allies are blue" rule).
- **Protagonist color coding**: Michael = blue, Franklin = green, Trevor = orange. Story mission and character-switch icons use these colors, and the icon for whichever protagonist you're currently playing is drawn larger than the other two.
- **Strangers and Freaks** side missions are marked with a **"?" (question-mark) icon**, tinted in the color of whichever protagonist can trigger it (blue/green/orange) — not a plain letter "S." Their name/identity is hidden until you first interact with them.

**Waypoints / navigation**
- **Purple** = a waypoint/route you set yourself on the map — drawn as a line on both the map and the radar, and the GPS silently recalculates if you go off-route (unlike GTA IV, it isn't voiced).
- **Yellow** = a route/waypoint the game itself sets for you during a mission.

**Wanted level & police**
- Wanted level runs on a **1–5 star scale** (reduced from 6 in earlier games).
- Roughly: 1 star = regular LSPD/Sheriff units; 2 stars = police shoot to kill, more cars; 3 stars = roadblocks/spike strips plus Police Mavericks with NOOSE units; 4 stars = heavier NOOSE/FIB response, up to 3 helicopters; 5 stars = maximum response, effectively map-wide, indefinite pursuit.
- Police don't use a fixed "search circle" like older games — each officer/unit has its own field of view and will chase you if you enter it.
- While hidden from active pursuers, the wanted stars **flash** and cone-shaped "search area" indicators appear on the radar showing where police are looking for you; the slower the stars flash, the closer you are to losing them.
- The radar's edge flashes red/blue when police currently have visual on you.

**Health / armor / special ability bars**
- Health is a bar along the left half of the space under the radar; it **flashes red** when critically low.
- Armor is a blue bar on the right half, next to/under health.
- The Special Ability meter (single-player only) shares that right-hand area with armor and is color-coded per protagonist (Michael/Franklin/Trevor); it fills with use and depletes while the ability is active.

**Weapon wheel**
- Hold the weapon-select input to open a radial wheel with roughly eight category slots (handguns, SMGs/machine guns, assault rifles, shotguns, sniper rifles, heavy weapons, melee, thrown).
- Each slot shows a colored line for the equipped weapon and its current ammo count; the wheel's center shows the weapon's name, its slot position/count within that category, and any attachments.
- Opening the wheel slows in-game time in story mode (this slowdown does not happen in GTA Online).

**Points of interest**
- Ammu-Nation, hospitals, garages, safehouses, and similar services each get their own fixed map/radar icon once discovered, distinct from mission blips — exact current icon art wasn't pinned down precisely in the sources checked (see notes).
